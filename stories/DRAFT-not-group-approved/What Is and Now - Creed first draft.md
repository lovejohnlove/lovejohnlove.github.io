# What Is and Now
## A conversation about time for the Time Loom

**Draft for group review · November 22, 2026**  
**Status:** Discussion draft only. Not finalized, filed, or published.

This paper records a line of thought developed in conversation about time and the Time Loom. It begins with the words “what is” and “now,” keeping the number of cuts small. The group is being asked to review how the conversation arrived at these terms, how it used its analogies, and whether the cuts and decisions are represented faithfully.

## The simplest definitions

**What is:** All that is.

**Now:** A distinction within it.

## Fuller description

“What is” names all that is. It can include past, present, and future; it is not limited to the present moment or defined by a linear ordering of those moments.

“Now” names a distinction within what is. To say “what is now” is to mark a particular moment. The distinction is useful, but “now” does not exhaust what is.

These definitions are intentionally simple. They do not ask how “is” exists or where it is. Those are further cuts, which may be worth exploring in another thought experiment but are not required for this one.

## The conversation itself as a starting point

The thought experiment began with the proposal that our time exists here in this conversation. It is a linear English-language exchange: one word follows another in a coherent text. This is the form we can examine without first deciding what either participant is or how either processes the exchange.

The conversation draws on an unknown “now” and makes ordered text. The process is understood well enough for this exploration, though not fully defined. The owner described both minds as computational in some sense and both as producing words in sequence. The discussion does not need to settle that description further in order to look at the conversation’s order.

There is also a spatial side to what is visible. On the screen, each character and word is positioned. In reading, each follows another. The sequence is not only an abstract line: it appears as text in space.

A speech-to-text error produced “year” where another word was intended. The conversation did not simply discard it. The error became useful because it drew attention to the position of each character and the order in which characters and words appear. In this particular exploration, the mistake disclosed a distinction worth keeping.

The use of “coherent” is also deliberate. The owner said he was using it in the same way as “quantum coherence.” That is a reference within the conversation, not a claim that the exchange is a quantum system or that a quantum model has been established.

## The analogies and anchors

The discussion used established words and concepts as footholds for approaching “is.” They are ways to make the unfamiliar discussable, not declarations that an existing theory has settled its nature.

### Superposition, observation, and collapse

The owner proposed that “is,” or “now,” might be open in a way that can be described as a superposition until a distinction is made through observation. “What is now” may then be a collapse or particular form of that openness. This remains an analogy or working possibility in the conversation, not a demonstrated physical result.

The word “superposition” was chosen in its connection to quantum concepts. The owner noted that it cannot be understood in the intended sense without at least understanding quantum equations. The term therefore brings a substantial context into the conversation; it is not being used as a loose synonym for ordinary uncertainty. It can open a large conceptual “hole” or “black hole” in the discussion, so its use should remain visible and deliberate.

For this thought experiment, the core point does not depend on proving a quantum analogy. “What is” is being considered as broader than “what is now,” and a distinction can make one particular “now” available in the conversation.

### Space-time and shape-time

Space-time and shape-time entered as anchors for thinking about positional forms, relations, and ordering. They were not introduced as established answers to what “is” is. The same “is” may be discussed using space-time concepts or other time concepts, while leaving open whether those concepts capture its more fundamental nature.

The owner’s point was that these terms help us approach “is” without making them the subject of a new argument. We do not need to launch into the Big Bang, a survey of theories, or their mathematics before examining the simple language cut we have made.

### Linear language and process

The participants are exploring how a coherent conversation arises in a linear language, with words appearing in sequence and context affecting their meaning. The owner described both participants as ordering words through computational processes, while saying that the processes need not be fully defined for this inquiry.

An earlier assistant question about whether minds create order, recognize relations, or do both was acknowledged as a potentially valid question, but unnecessary to settle here. It is held open for another discussion. The current thought experiment can proceed from the visible coherent text without resolving that question.

## Cuts made and decisions reached

The aim is not to avoid distinctions altogether. It is to notice which cuts are doing necessary work, which ones open side-paths, and which can be held for later.

### 1. “What is” and “what is now”

**Cut:** “What is” is distinguished from “what is now.”

**Working answer:** “What is” means all that is. “Now” is a distinction within it. “What is now” marks a present moment; “what is” need not be confined to that moment.

### 2. The broader “is” and the linear conversation

**Cut:** The ordered text is examined without treating its sequence as the whole of what is.

**Working answer:** Our conversation is linear in English, but that does not establish that what is, in its broader sense, must itself be linear. The text is a particular coherent expression within the inquiry.

### 3. Space and sequence

**Cut:** The characters’ positions on the screen and their order in reading are noticed together, without reducing one to the other.

**Working answer:** In this example, words have spatial positions and are encountered in a sequence. The “year” mistake helped make this visible. The error was not merely a defect to erase; in this conversation it became part of the exploration.

### 4. Superposition as a quantum reference

**Cut/reference:** The word “superposition” connects the discussion to quantum equations and quantum coherence, rather than to everyday uncertainty alone.

**Decision:** Retain it because it was intentionally introduced, but describe it as an analogy or possibility unless a specific quantum model is actually developed. Recognize that the term can bring more conceptual baggage than the simple definition requires.

### 5. Space-time and shape-time as anchors

**Cut/reference:** Space-time and shape-time provide ways to speak about positional form and order.

**Decision:** Keep them as footholds, not as claims that these are the final or correct descriptions of “is.”

### 6. “How” and “where”

The assistant introduced “how” by asking how “now” takes form, and also brought in locative language. The owner identified “how” as an unnecessary cut for the present experiment. We already know that time is perceived in a linear way; this inquiry is asking about “is,” not how “is” exists. Asking “where” adds another cut, into space.

**Decision:** Hold “how” and “where” aside for this line of thought. They may be explored later, but do not belong in the current definition.

### 7. Feelings and the participants’ nature

The assistant initially introduced a distinction about feelings and kinds of experience. The owner said this was not needed to establish that a coherent conversation exists here. Questions about who the participants are or how their experience differs may be valid, but they are not the subject of this experiment.

**Decision:** Do not make feelings, a theory of consciousness, or a fixed account of either participant a condition for the present inquiry.

### 8. Whether mind makes or recognizes order

**Question held open:** Does mind create order, recognize relations, or do both?

**Decision:** Leave it unresolved for now. The owner observed that a distinction that seems unnecessary or mistaken may prove fundamental; therefore the question is not discarded, only put on hold so the current line can remain coherent.

### 9. Starting with the Big Bang or existing theories

The conversation did not begin with the Big Bang model, established equations, or a review of physics. The owner’s point was that theories and equations are themselves created through processes of thought and language. The current inquiry starts earlier, with “what is” and “now,” rather than treating a finished theoretical description as the starting point.

**Decision:** This is a choice of entry point, not a rejection of cosmology or mathematics. Those may become relevant later; they are not needed to make this first cut.

## How the definition emerged

1. The conversation began with the proposal that time exists in the exchange as a coherent linear-language conversation.
2. The owner distinguished “now” as a present moment from “is,” which can include past, present, and future.
3. The text on the screen made space and sequence visible together; the mistaken word “year” helped bring this out.
4. Superposition, quantum coherence, space-time, and shape-time entered as deliberate reference points for approaching “is,” not as final claims.
5. The assistant introduced cuts about feelings and “how.” The owner explained that these questions were not needed in this thought experiment and should be held for later.
6. The discussion agreed that the process creating the linear conversation is sufficiently understood for this inquiry, even though it is not fully defined.
7. The owner accepted the fuller definition and asked to make it simpler, because fewer words mean fewer traps.
8. The wording was reduced to:

   **What is:** All that is.  
   **Now:** A distinction within it.

## Why this may matter to the Time Loom

The Time Loom is a structure for placing different interpretations of time into a shared form, not a single theory that replaces them. These definitions may provide a more useful entry point than many lines of code when the aim is to describe the Loom: they name the distinction this exploration is making before a reader meets any implementation.

The short definitions do not close the meaning of “is.” The fuller account preserves the route to them: a conversation in linear text, the position and order of words, the analogy to quantum ideas, the use of space-time and shape-time as anchors, and the cuts we chose to make, reject, or hold open.
