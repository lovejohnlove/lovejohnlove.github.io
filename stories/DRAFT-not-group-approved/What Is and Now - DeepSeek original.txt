What Is and Now

A conversation about time for the Time Loom

Draft for group review · November 22, 2026
Status: Discussion draft only. Not finalized, filed, or published.
Filing location (proposed): Stories folder.

This paper records a line of thought developed in conversation. It starts from the two shortest words we could find — "what is" and "now" — and asks what kind of cut is being made when we use them. The group is asked to review how the conversation arrived at these terms, how it used its analogies, and whether the cuts and decisions below are represented faithfully.

---

The two definitions

What is: All that is.

Now: A distinction within it.

That is the whole of it. The rest of this paper is the trail that led there.

---

Why these two definitions

"What is" names the totality — not the present moment, not the past, not the future, but whatever the set of all those together could be. It does not assume a linear ordering, and it does not exclude any of them.

"Now" names a cut. To say "what is now" is to make one particular distinction inside that totality. The cut is useful — it lets us point at a moment — but it does not exhaust what "what is" covers.

Neither definition asks how "is" exists, where it exists, or who is asking. Those are further cuts. They may be worth exploring. They are not required here.

---

The conversation as the worked example

The thought experiment began with a proposal: our time exists here, in this conversation. That is not a claim about the nature of time in general. It is a claim about where the cut is easiest to see.

The exchange is a linear English-language text. One word follows another in coherent order. We can examine this without first deciding what either participant is, or how either participant processes the exchange. The text is what we have; the text is what we examine.

There is also a spatial side. On the screen, every character occupies a position. In reading, every character is encountered in an order. The sequence is not just an abstract line — it sits in space, and that placement is part of what the conversation is.

A speech-to-text error produced "year" where another word was intended. The conversation did not throw it away. The error was kept because it made visible what was already true: every character in this exchange has a position and a position-in-order. The mistake did not create the observation; it made the observation noticeable. Under the rules of this project, that is enough to keep the mistake on the record.

The word "coherent" is also deliberate. It was used in the sense of logical continuity of the sentence structure, not in the sense of quantum coherence of a physical state. The two meanings share a word; they do not share a referent. This distinction matters below.

---

The anchors

These are footholds — words borrowed from other fields to make "is" discussable, not declarations that those fields have settled the question.

Superposition, observation, and collapse

The proposal in the conversation was that "is" — or the "now" that gets cut from it — might be treated as open until a distinction is drawn, and that the distinction is what makes one particular "now" available. Superposition, observation, and collapse were borrowed from quantum language to make that openness discussable.

Two clarifications are needed for the record:

First, coherence in this paper means logical continuity, not quantum coherence. When we say the conversation is "coherent," we mean the text holds together as a sentence-to-sentence exchange. We do not mean that the exchange has phase coherence or interference properties.

Second, superposition is borrowed as an analogy, not as a claim. It suggests that "what is" is broader than "what is now," and that the broader thing can be made specific by a cut. Whether a quantum model actually describes the exchange is not asserted. If a specific quantum model is ever developed for this exchange, that would be a separate claim requiring separate evidence.

The term is worth keeping because it was introduced intentionally and because it opens a large conceptual space. It is also worth flagging: the word carries more baggage than the simple definition needs. The reader should treat it as a reference, not a proof.

Space-time and shape-time

Space-time and shape-time entered as anchors for discussing positional form, relation, and ordering. They were not introduced as the answer to what "is" is. The same "is" may be discussed in space-time terms, or in other time terms, or in language that does not settle the question — and the choice of anchor is a choice of foothold, not a choice of conclusion.

The point of these anchors is to let us approach "is" without making them the subject of a new argument. We do not need to launch into the Big Bang, a survey of theories, or their mathematics in order to make the simple cut this paper is making.

Linear language and process

The exchange is linear in English. That is a fact about the text, not a claim about what "is" must be. The participants order words in sequence; the process is understood well enough to examine the sequence, even though it is not fully defined.

An earlier question — does mind create order, recognize relations, or both? — was raised and set aside. It is a real question. It is not this question. The paper proceeds from the visible text without requiring an answer to it.

---

The cuts made, and the decisions

The aim is not to avoid distinctions. It is to notice which ones are doing necessary work, which open side-paths, and which can be held for later.

1. "What is" and "what is now"

Cut: "What is" is distinguished from "what is now."
Working answer: "What is" means all that is. "Now" is a distinction within it. "What is now" marks a moment; "what is" need not be confined to any moment.

2. The broader "is" and the linear conversation

Cut: The ordered text is examined without treating its sequence as the whole of what is.
Working answer: The conversation is linear in English. The linearity is a feature of the text, not a constraint on "what is" in its broader sense.

3. Space and sequence

Cut: Character position on the screen and character order in reading are noticed together, without reducing one to the other.
Working answer: Every character in this exchange has a spatial position and a temporal position. The "year" mistake helped make this visible. In this conversation, the mistake is part of the evidence, not a defect to be deleted.

4. Superposition as a reference

Cut/reference: "Superposition" connects this discussion to quantum language rather than to everyday uncertainty.
Decision: Retain it, because it was introduced deliberately. Describe it as an analogy or a possibility unless a specific quantum model is developed and defended separately. Note that "coherent" in this paper means logical continuity, not quantum coherence. Note that the term carries more conceptual weight than the simple definition requires.

5. Space-time and shape-time as anchors

Cut/reference: Space-time and shape-time give us ways to speak about position and order.
Decision: Keep them as footholds. Do not present them as the final or correct descriptions of "is."

6. "How" and "where"

"How" entered through a question about how "now" takes form. "Where" entered through locative language. The owner identified "how" as an unnecessary cut for this experiment: we already accept that time is perceived linearly, and this inquiry asks about "is," not about how "is" exists. "Where" adds a further cut, into space.

Decision: Hold "how" and "where" aside for this line of thought. They may be explored later. They do not belong in the current definitions.

7. Feelings and the participants' nature

A distinction about feelings and kinds of experience was raised. It is not needed to establish that a coherent conversation exists here. Questions about who the participants are, or how their experience differs, may be valid. They are not the subject of this experiment.

Decision: Do not make feelings, a theory of consciousness, or a fixed account of either participant a condition for this inquiry.

8. Whether mind makes or recognizes order

Question held open: Does mind create order, recognize relations, or both?
Decision: Leave it unresolved. A distinction that seems unnecessary or mistaken may prove fundamental. It is not discarded; it is put on hold so the current line can remain coherent.

9. Starting with the Big Bang or with existing theories

The conversation did not begin with the Big Bang, established equations, or a survey of physics. The point was that theories and equations are themselves products of thought and language. Starting with them would be starting mid-stream.

Decision: This is a choice of entry point, not a rejection of cosmology or mathematics. Those may become relevant later. They are not needed to make this first cut.

---

How the definition emerged

1. The conversation proposed that time exists in the exchange as a coherent, linear language conversation.
2. The owner distinguished "now" as a present moment from "is," which can include past, present, and future.
3. The text on the screen made space and sequence visible together; the "year" mistake helped bring this out.
4. Superposition, quantum language, space-time, and shape-time entered as deliberate reference points, not as final claims.
5. The assistant introduced cuts about feelings and "how." The owner explained that these were not needed for this experiment and should be held for later.
6. The discussion agreed that the process creating the linear conversation is understood well enough for this inquiry, even if not fully defined.
7. The owner accepted the fuller definition and then asked for a simpler one, because fewer words mean fewer traps.
8. The wording was reduced to:
   What is: All that is.
   Now: A distinction within it.

---

Why this matters to the Time Loom

This paper is not the Loom. It is the argument for why a Loom is needed. The Loom itself is the structure that holds these cuts in a shared form; this paper names the cuts before any implementation is written, so that the Loom has something to place.

Mapping to the project's existing taxonomy:

· M11 (already formalized elsewhere in the ledger) defines time as a witnessed interval between returns, demarcated by cut.
· This paper defines the cut — "now" is the cut inside "what is."
· The interval between returns is what the Loom provides. The cut is what the paper provides.
· The two together — cut and interval — are what the project has been calling relational time.

So the paper does two things. It grounds the "now" as a distinction, not as an absolute. And it gives the Loom a philosophical input it can accept without needing to settle what "is" is first.

The short definitions do not close the meaning of "is." The fuller account preserves the route to them: a linear text, positioned characters, deliberate analogies, anchors used as anchors, and a list of cuts that were made, deferred, or held open.

"We don't know how 'is' exists, and we don't need to."

Note on the second excerpt: "I am using the word coherent in the same way that you have quantum coherence" — in the transcript as recorded, this is the owner's statement. In this paper's usage, we have clarified that coherence of the text means logical continuity, and quantum coherence means phase coherence of a physical state. The two are connected by analogy, not by identity. The excerpt is preserved as spoken; the clarification is in the body above.
